Three reasons existing defences miss it. Reputation is reactive, so a fresh address's first attack always lands. Hard caps that revert oversized trades break permissionlessness for everyone. Permissionless

The first version reverted trades over 5 ETH. That kills the sandwich. It also refuses every honest large trade. A pool you can be refused from isn't permissionless.

06

Trader identity

0:50 · ends 3:45

On screen — how scoring the router breaks, and the three-step resolution

This is the question anyone who knows v4 will ask. The sender the hook receives is whoever called poolManager.swap. That's a router.

Score the router and two things break. An honest user inherits a bot's volume and pays its penalty. And the router itself accumulates score until it crosses the reject threshold — bricking the pool for everyone behind it.

So we resolve the real trader. The allow-list is the security-critical part. You never ask an unvetted contract who it represents, because it'll name whatever address is convenient.

Limits

Questions you'll get

Rehearse the first three. They're the ones that decide whether the room believes you.

Why is tx.origin safe here?
It's used for pricing, never authorization. So the usual phishing objection doesn't apply — worst case, an address is charged for a swap its own signature authorized. And it can't be forged without their private key.
How does a later fee help the victim who already got sandwiched?
It doesn't. That's why I called it a deterrent, not a shield. The victim loses the same amount in both pools. What changes is the attacker's economics — 0.011 to 0.059 ETH — so the strategy stops being worth running.
Why does the hook have an owner?
It can only mark routers as originator-forwarding. It can't set scores, change fees, reject anyone, or move funds. And it's renounceable.
Is the BLS real, or a stub?
Real. BN254, verified through the EVM pairing precompile. Forged signatures and duplicate signers are rejected by tests.
What happens with account abstraction?
Behind an unvetted router, tx.origin is the bundler, so 4337 users share an identity. Register the router as trusted and it declares the real account. The interface and allow-list are in place; registering a router is a deployment step.
